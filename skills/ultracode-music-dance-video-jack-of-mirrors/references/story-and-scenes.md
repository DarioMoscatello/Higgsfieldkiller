# Jack of Mirrors: story and scenes in detail

How the earlier "Jack of Mirrors" film was built, scene by scene and hit by hit. Use it as the plan for this concept,
and fix the weak spots in section G.

## A. Building the concept

**1. Start from a structural fact, not the nouns.** The earlier concept came from the title: two reflections = a half
turn = the symmetry of a court card (a figure joined to its upside-down twin). One fact then supplied the hero image, the
mirror floor, the audience, the ending and a visual pun. Test: can the whole story be drawn with one object or rule?

**2. Pitch several, then decide honestly.** Five independent pitches from different lenses (formal rule about the frame;
dancer first; three-act story; type and symbols as performers; 3D world and camera journey) plus the director's own,
scored by three judges (creative, animation, technical) on originality, song fit, dance/story, feasibility and polish
ceiling. Useful output: the grafts and the risks, not the ranking (the top two differed by one point in 150). The
director's own pitch, as written, ranked last: "a synopsis, not a score", with no hit times. The fix was a brief with exact
hit times per scene. Decide before the panel whether it chooses or only adds; do not build first and then ask.

**3. The court card is the song's natural reading.** Three of six pitches independently found it, and five of six
finished videos of this song used cracks mended with gold. What made the earlier film its own was the specific twists
on top: the reflection's shrinking lag, the per-bar world swap, words only in the gap, the fingertips that touch on the
last kick, and the card flipping to its back with the title. Keep those, and add your own on the same spine.

## B. Story devices that worked

**4. One variable that moves one way.** The reflection's lag behind the hero: 0.8 s (a whole beat) in hook 1, 0.4 s in the
quiet verse, 0.2 s after the drop, easing to 0 over the break, unison afterwards. One function of `t` that every scene reads.
Hold each move at least a beat so a late answer reads as an answer. Hide unavoidable jumps of a piecewise value behind hard
cuts, and say so in the brief.

**5. A counter on the bar line that pays off on a lyric.** One new mark per verse downbeat (8 marks for 8 bars), then the
marks transformed over the line they belong to. The picture changes by a predictable amount every 3.2 s: escalation without
new objects.

**6. Plant a promise, pay it on the last hit.** Two fingertips stop either side of a line and do not touch in hook 1, again
in hook 2, again with nine figures; on the last kick they touch. Make "near" and "touch" two named constants so an edit cannot
close the gap by accident.

**7. One routine, escalated.** One four-bar hook routine (one bar per hook line), danced as a duet in hook 2, then by a row
of five, nine in canon, a giant at about 3.2× scale, back to one. Same poses and hit times, so it is recognised on the third
pass.

**8. Make silence visible.** Hits stop for 1.8 s before the drop: stop all motion, strings slack, figure limp, light down to
one cone, one slow push, and one small series of events on the sixteenth grid (four marks, one per sixteenth). The last
event before the silence lands on the last hat.

**9. One colour follows one layer.** "Gold = bass": foil dims whenever the bass rests (rests measured on the bass layer),
flares 0.26 on each bass note with a 0.17 s half-life, drains when bass leaves, slams back for the length of each lone low
hit.

**10. Lone hits as events with a body.** First two drum hits: knocks on a glass surface, a jolt toward the camera, a star of
cracks that doubles on the second. Lone kicks of the break: strings snap with loose ends and sparks; the next downbeat turns
the whole frame about a pin.

**11. Counting devices from the grid.** The quiet verse has exactly 56 sixteenth slots; 56 cards were dealt, one per tick, the
last on the last hat, with a numeral counting them and tally strokes struck through in fives.

## C. Scene structure

**12. Scenes follow sections; cuts on bar lines; a first/last-frame contract.** Nine scenes, every boundary a hard cut on a
bar line, one scene active per frame:

| Scene | Time | Bars | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0-12.8 | 0-3 | Hook 1, no drums |
| 2 | 12.8-25.6 | 4-7 | Quiet verse, ending in the 1.8 s stillness |
| 3 | 25.6-32.0 | 8-9 | The drop |
| 4 | 32.0-38.4 | 10-11 | Loud verse |
| 5 | 38.4-44.8 | 12-13 | Loud verse |
| 6 | 44.8-51.2 | 14-15 | Loud verse (bar 14 thinner) |
| 7 | 51.2-54.4 | 16 | Break: lone hits |
| 8 | 54.4-67.2 | 17-20 | Hook 2, loudest |
| 9 | 67.2-79.17 | 21-24 | Hook 3 and the hard stop |

Give each scene an exact first and last frame so neighbours can be written independently and still cut cleanly (scene 1
ends on a warm flash at full strength on its last frame; scene 2 starts on the same flash fading out in 0.25 s). Every scene
starts on a clearly different shot size from the previous scene's last.

**13. Escalation across the song** (light, figure, camera, story state per section): one pool of light and a bust on a card →
a lit room and the full figure on strings → maximum light on the drop and the widest shot, then ever closer, one leap per bar →
dark and bare in the break → the room at maximum, unison, many shots per bar → a hall of mirrors (1, 5, 9, a giant, back to 1),
contact, the end card.

## D. Tying images to hits

**14. The per-bar signature.** The earlier film used the song's per-bar drop-out (snare to next kick, 0.6 s): the world
switches look on the snare frame, the figure takes off on the snare, hangs, lands on the kick, and the words the voice sings
alone appear as lettering. Rules from its brief: anything moving around him is suspended at the top of its arc while he
hangs and comes down with him; **the landing kick is the bar's punch line** (landing, a thud via a camera shake and zoom
punch, a bloom pulse, a floor ripple, and the bar's image arriving with the lyric's key word).

**15. One readable image per bar on its kick.** Each image is a single object transformed by a physical hinge the viewer
knows, so a still before and after the kick tells the story: figures topple outward one per sixteenth and slam flat on the
kick; cards spin about their vertical axis and come round as a different card; fallen objects rise like lanterns one per
sixteenth; a wall of cards turns edge-on in the gap and nine tall cards slam down as bars; a letterbox slams in over 3
frames and a credit roll rises, then jams (judders every sixteenth, gets nowhere); crop brackets close in and the figure
breaks out; shapes flip upside down to become other shapes; a letter and its mirror image read as another letter.

**16. Give every repetition of the signature its own composition.** Hook 2 had a 20-entry shot list, each gap a poster: low
and frontal; a two-shot across the mirror line with words either side; three-quarter with words coming at the lens; top-down
with words written flat on the mirror. [mistake: the four gap shots of the first loud hook looked alike; decide one named
composition, lettering position and camera relation per instance in the brief, and compare thumbnails side by side]

**17. Steady loops on the tick, not the word.** A character bobs on every hat and snaps upright on a snare; a crowd claps on
every hat for a set span and stops dead on a sixteenth.

**18. Lettering as a whitelist.** A fixed phrase list per bar in code; only cut-in times come from the word timings (fallback:
even spread), capped so the phrase is fully up 0.3 s into its window. In the earlier film lettering was ink on black,
invisible in one look and bright in the other, so the look switch was also what made words legible.

## E. The ending

**19. Hang the last 1.5 s on the last four hits and hold.** The audio stops mid-line at full level. The earlier ending:
kick 78.2 (frame 2346) contact, a bright point, one ripple, a punch in on the hands; snare 78.4 the picture is revealed to be
a card face; kick 78.6 it flips over in 3 frames; kick 78.8 (frame 2364) the title stamps in with a zoom punch
(`1 + 0.022·punch`), shake and bloom over 0.24 s; frames 2364-2374 a dead hold with only a slow shimmer; no fade.
Nothing may be scheduled after the last real hit, or it is missed by frames.

**20. Before the end, collapse the multiples.** Nine figures slide into one in 4 frames on a kick, panes fold shut, the camera
steps in twice on the next hits, the background turns over in a column wave.

## F. Process

**21. A per-scene brief with hit times is what lets scenes be built in parallel** (by you in sequence, or by helpers):
the idea; the music table with exact hit times; the engine API; "things people get wrong" (stateless scenes, reset order,
coordinate and pose conventions, key colours); the scene contract
`export default { id, t0, t1, init(stage, kit), update(t, stage, kit) }`; what each author may and may not edit; render
commands; the cut list with first and last frames; then one section per scene, bar by bar.

**22. Put the review in the middle.** Build, one fresh look at every bar on sheets, fix, then the full render. The earlier
session left the review for last and never did it.

## G. Weak spots the earlier film shipped, with what to do instead

| Weak spot | Do instead |
|---|---|
| Performer small in wide shots (widest z 15.4; a troupe at distance 9-11) | A minimum figure height (about 250 px of 1080) except in one designed pull-back; a medium solo on the landing kick every second bar; wide shots only in the gap; give the lead a unique colour in crowds; check at 480 px wide |
| A bright ivory card wall behind the dancer | Reserve the lightest value for the hero; background mid or dark; a value gap of about 4.5:1 between figure and background; a dark outline or lit rim; threshold a sheet to test silhouettes |
| Flashes and big letters near blown-out (several boosts stacked on one frame) | One boost source per hit, cap the sum, check the brightest frame's histogram (share of pixels above 250) |
| 2.5 s mostly black (a crop sequence) | Keep something alive inside the black; let the window move; halve the black time |
| Look-alike repetitions of the signature | Item 16 |
